Origonese:
The Universal Language of Light
Lesson 2:
Written By Dylan Kutz
 Review: Obololo! Glad ori decided to learn Lesson 2. Have ori already been calling fruits and vegetables o-leaf-lo? Have ori told ori’s Omomolo that ori o-lovey-lo her more than the whole o-wiggly-wo? Great! Let’s begin.

The Origonese word for happy or giddy is: O-hapi-lo (Oh-happy-loh) – or- O-hapi-lolo for super happy and giddy.
To use in a sentence: Ori is very o-hapi-lo today! How about you, ori?

A new Origonese word to respond to question: Ori is very o-hapi-lo also. It’s o-drippy-wo (raining) ori o-lovey-lo when it o-drippy-wo’s (rains). 
Origonese Phonetics: oh-drip-ee-woah or oh-drip-ee-woah’s
Origonese word for rain: O-drippy-wo. (Oh-drip-ee-woah)

Used in a sentence: Ori doesn’t o-likey-lo (like) the o-drippy-wo. 
Origonese Definition of o-likey-lo: like or to like something
Origonese Phonetics: oh-like-ee-loh

Origonese word for eat: O-munchy-lo
Origonese phonetics: Oh-munch-ee-loh
Used in a sentence: Hey Ori! Let’s o-munchy-lo some o-leaf-lo!
