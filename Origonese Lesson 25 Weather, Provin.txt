Origonese Lesson 25: Weather, Provinces, Demographics, and Expressive Farewells

Welcome to Lesson 25 of Origonese! In this lesson, we are expanding our vocabulary into a comprehensive set of daily topics: checking the weather across different provinces and cities, classifying life stages from fledgling to adept, talking about currency, and sending people off with expressive, rhyming cultural farewells.

Part 1: Weather Conditions (Oweathy-wo)

When looking out at the sky, use these terms to describe the atmosphere:
•	It’s raining = Its o-drippy-wo
•	It’s snowing = Its o-flakey-wo
•	It’s stormy = Its o-crazy-wow
•	It’s cloudy = Its o-shadey-wow
•	It’s sunny = Its o-shiny-wo
•	It’s windy = Its o-blowy-wo
•	It’s nice weather = Its o-pleasy-wow weathy-wo
•	It’s bad weather = Its o-negativy-wow weathy-wo
•	It’s hot = Its o-burny-wow
•	It’s cold = Its o-nippy-wow

Part 2: Geography — Provinces and Safe Zones (Realms)
In the Origonese cultural landscape, major regions and cities have their own special designations:
•	Canada = Kanaja
•	Alberta = Alibija (A-Provy) | Edmonton = Etijintin (E-Safe) | Calgary = Ecalajin (C-Safe)
•	British Columbia = Briji Kojumbio (B-Provy) | Vancouver = Vankujo (V-Safe)
•	Saskatchewan = Sisijawinajin (S-Provy) | Regina = Rijini (R-Safe)
•	Manitoba = Jinitoba (J-Provy) | Winnipeg = Jinnijig (J-Safe)
•	Ontario = Oritarion (O-Provy) | Toronto = Tojinto (T-Safe)
•	Nova Scotia = Jovo Scojio (JS-Provy) | Halifax = Jilifax (X-Safe)
•	United States = Unito Realms (Ajerika) | New York = Jiwi Yorky (Y-Safe) | Los Angeles = Losty Angelos (A-Safe) | Miami = Jiaji (I-Safe)

Part 3: Life Stages & Demographics
Origonese divides life and community roles into distinct titles. (Note: There are no specific words for boy or girl; everyone belongs to these broader life chapters, in Origonese they see no difference between a boy and man and woman and girl age and gender don’t matter in Origonese just gender is specified as o-mon or o-won a boy is born an o-won its not something he has to ‘grow up’ into and accomplish and earn his manhood it is already given freely).
•	Baby (0 to 1 years) = Fledgling or greenhorn = O-fledgy-wo or O-greeny-wow
•	Preteen (11 and 12 years) = Intermediaries = Intermy-wo’s
•	Teenager (13 to 17 years) = Ninja = O-ninjy-wo
•	Adult (18 to 64 years) = Givers = O-givy-wo
•	Senior (65+ years) = Adept = O-adepty-wo
•	Woman = O-won
•	Man = O-mon

Part 4: Currency & Terms of Endearment
•	Dollars = Dilly’s = O-dilly-bars
•	Jiggo = A term of endearment for someone special.

Part 5: The Master Index of Farewells (Owobolo System)
Depending on the energy you want to leave someone with, pick the matching farewell:
•	Owobolo! = Goodbye, have a wobbly sparkly day!
•	Owobogo! = Goodbye, have a bouncy bubbly day!
•	Owoboro! = Goodbye, have a crazy, hazy with harmony day!
•	Owobobo! = Goodbye, have an introspective, well protected day!
•	Owobodo! = Goodbye, stay safe, play safe, and make no mistakes!
•	Owoboko! = Goodbye, have a blessed and well dressed day!
•	Owoboso! = Goodbye, have a stress free, mess free day!
•	Owoboxo! = Goodbye, have a box, and locks free day!
•	Owobozo! = Goodbye, have a zen, full of gems day!
•	Owobovo! = Goodbye, have a day full of visions and wisdom!
•	Owobowo! = Goodbye, have a waitless and painless day!
•	Owobojo! = Goodbye, have a jumpy, grumpy free day!
•	Owoboto! = Goodbye, have a youthful, truthful day!
•	Owoboyo! = Goodbye, have a mellow, yellow day!
•	Owobopo! = Goodbye, have a polite, bright day!
•	Owobofo! = Goodbye, have a fair, aware day!
•	Owobono! = Goodbye, have a never ending day!

Practice Exercise for Lesson 25:
Try translating this short scenario using your new vocabulary: “In E-Safe (Edmonton), it is o-nippy-wow and o-flakey-wo today. An o-givy-wo adult is walking to the store with a few dilly-bars in their pocket.”
Which farewell from the Owobolo list would you give to a friend heading out into the snow?
